# Weekly Market Report: 2026/3/24 ~ 2026/3/30

**Report Date:** March 31, 2026, 4:25 PM JST

  

## **_1. Macroeconomic Conditions and US Market_**

  

### **Summary**

The week of March 24-30, 2026 marked a turbulent period for US equity markets as escalating conflict in the Middle East dominated investor sentiment and drove significant volatility across asset classes. The S&P 500 fell 3.24% during the week, closing at 6,343.72 on March 30, its lowest level since August [(1)](bbg://screens/NSN%20TCQ9ISKK3NYA). The benchmark index posted its fifth consecutive weekly decline, the longest such streak since 2022 [(2)](bbg://screens/NSN%20TCKOSNKJH6V7). The selloff accelerated on Friday, March 27, when the VIX closed above 30 for the first time since April, reaching 31.01 [(3)](bbg://screens/NSN%20TCKQH4KIUPSE). The Nasdaq 100 entered correction territory on March 27, falling more than 10% from its October peak [(4)](bbg://screens/NSN%20TC7HNNKJH6V7).

The primary catalyst for market weakness was the ongoing Iran war and its impact on global energy markets. Brent crude oil jumped to $112 per barrel as Iran continued to restrict passage through the Strait of Hormuz [(5)](bbg://screens/NSN%20TCKTKHKIJHCP). By the end of the week, Brent hit $115 per barrel [(6)](bbg://screens/NSN%20TCPFUWKK3NY8). This surge in oil prices sparked fears that elevated energy costs would reignite inflation and potentially derail the Federal Reserve's progress toward its 2% target. Goldman Sachs warned that under a worst-case scenario, US inflation could peak at 4.9% [(7)](bbg://screens/NSN%20TCEMPBBQ99TT).

Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell provided crucial guidance on March 30, stating that longer-term inflation expectations appear to be "well anchored beyond the short term" [(8)](bbg://screens/NSN%20TCPYYKT96OSN). Powell emphasized that the central bank has little control over supply shocks like the war-driven surge in oil prices [(9)](bbg://screens/NSN%20TCPZNZT96OSP). His comments prompted a significant shift in market expectations, with traders cutting the odds of a Fed rate hike in 2026 to about 25% from around 35% on Friday [(10)](bbg://screens/NSN%20TCPFUWKK3NY8). Treasury yields rallied following Powell's remarks, with the 10-year yield declining eight basis points to 4.34% on March 30 [(11)](bbg://screens/NSN%20TCQ8FWKK3NYA).

Consumer sentiment deteriorated sharply during the period. The University of Michigan's final March sentiment index fell to 53.3 from a preliminary reading of 55.5, with year-ahead inflation expectations jumping as gasoline prices climbed [(12)](bbg://screens/NSN%20TCK9NNT9NJLZ). US business activity growth also cooled to an almost one-year low in March, with the S&P Global flash composite index falling to 51.4 [(13)](bbg://screens/NSN%20TCEOZVKGZAQI). A Bloomberg survey of economists revised their 2026 PCE price index forecast to 3.1% from 2.6% in the prior survey [(14)](bbg://screens/NSN%20TCIVEAD2BTHC).

Political developments added to market uncertainty. The Wall Street Journal reported on March 31 that President Donald Trump told aides he's willing to end the US military campaign against Iran even if the Strait of Hormuz remains largely closed, sparking a rebound in equity futures [(15)](bbg://screens/NSN%20TCQU6BKGIFQJ). Morgan Stanley strategists suggested the S&P 500 correction was approaching its final stages, noting that the forward price-to-earnings ratio had compressed by 17%, similar to other growth scares that did not result in recession [(16)](bbg://screens/NSN%20TCP7IBT96OSN).

### **Fed Watch & Central Bank Commentary**

Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell delivered pivotal remarks on March 30 at Harvard University, stating that inflation expectations remain "well anchored beyond the short term" [(6)](bbg://screens/NSN%20TCPYYKT96OSN). Powell emphasized that President Donald Trump's sweeping tariffs resulted in a one-time price bump and that the central bank has little control over supply shocks such as the war-driven surge in oil prices [(7)](bbg://screens/NSN%20TCPWXIT96OSI). Following Powell's comments, traders reversed course to bet again on a Fed rate cut this year, with money markets cutting the odds of a Federal Reserve rate hike in 2026 to about 25% from around 35% on Friday [(8)](bbg://screens/NSN%20TCPFUWKK3NY8).

Federal Reserve Bank of New York President John Williams said interest rates were well positioned amid signs of significant supply chain disruptions due to war in the Middle East [(9)](bbg://screens/NSN%20TCQF55KK3NY8). Williams noted that the labor market has some slack and is not adding to inflation [(10)](bbg://screens/NSN%20TCQFNNNPYH35). Federal Reserve Bank of Richmond President Tom Barkin said the US-Israel war in Iran threatens to add to already elevated inflationary pressures and that progress on inflation "may be at risk of stalling" [(11)](bbg://screens/NSN%20TCK7XPKJH6V4). Federal Reserve Bank of Philadelphia President Anna Paulson said the surge in commodity prices driven by war in the Middle East held more risk for the US economy due to the fact that inflation has been elevated for years [(12)](bbg://screens/NSN%20TCINGAKJH6V5).

Natixis economists revised their Fed forecast, pushing back the next rate cut to July from their previous forecast [(13)](bbg://screens/NSN%20TCKKQHT9NJLV).

The sustained elevation of the VIX above 30 indicated that investors expected continued volatility ahead, particularly as the Iran conflict showed no signs of resolution and oil prices remained elevated.

### **Yield Curve Analysis**

The 10-year minus 2-year spread widened slightly during the week, moving from 47.3 basis points on March 24 to 52.0 basis points on March 30, representing a steepening of 4.7 basis points. This modest steepening occurred despite the overall decline in yields across the curve, as the 2-year yield fell more sharply than the 10-year. The yield curve remained positively sloped, indicating that the inversion that had characterized much of the previous tightening cycle had fully reversed.

The steepening pattern suggests that markets were pricing in a more dovish Fed policy path in the near term while maintaining concerns about longer-term inflation risks from elevated oil prices. The 10-year yield reached an eight-month high on March 27 before retreating following Powell's comments [(1)](bbg://screens/NSN%20TCK1CYPV4WE8). The curve dynamics reflected competing forces: near-term recession fears driven by the oil shock versus longer-term inflation concerns. For bank profitability, the positively sloped curve remained supportive of net interest margins, though the modest steepening during the week was insufficient to provide a significant boost to financial sector earnings.

The interplay between fixed income, currency, and equity markets during the week revealed complex cross-asset dynamics driven by the Iran conflict. The sharp reversal in yields on March 30, with the 10-year falling eight basis points to 4.34% [(2)](bbg://screens/NSN%20TCQ8FWKK3NYA), provided some relief to growth stocks but came too late to prevent significant weekly losses.
